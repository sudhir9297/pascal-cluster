# Bath Space

A procedural bathroom furniture plugin registered in the adjacent Pascal editor on `plugin-testing`.

Choose **Freestanding**, **Wall-mounted**, or **Corner** in the Bath Space sidebar. Freestanding and wall-mounted designs include Modern, Shaker, Fluted, and Open Console. Press **Place** and click in the scene; wall-mounted vanities require a wall. Changing the type or design during placement updates the preview. Select the cabinet and expand its inspector to customize it; the blue-purple scene arrows resize its dimensions.

Freestanding and wall-mounted vanities include:

- Drawer stacks, doors with shelves, mixed storage, drawers on either side, and an open console layout.
- Adjustable drawer rows and columns with equal-height or shallow-top drawers. Drawer boxes include solid rectangular bottoms, side walls, backs, and slide rails. The cabinet has a solid back panel without plumbing cutouts.
- **Individual opening:** click a drawer or door front to animate just that part. Select a vanity and press E to open or close everything together. Clicking or pressing E during an animation reverses it. Opening sliders control all drawers or all doors.
- **Custom sections:** choose Custom sections under Storage to start from the existing layout. Use up to three sections arranged left to right; choose drawers, doors, or open shelves for each. Adjust section width proportions, individual drawer height proportions, drawer counts, door counts, and shelf counts. Dimensions remain bounded by the overall cabinet size.
- Flat, Shaker, or fluted fronts; inset or overlay mounting; adjustable gaps, panel thickness, frame width, and flute spacing.
- Bar pulls, round knobs, edge pulls, or handleless fronts.
- Freestanding models have square, tapered, or round legs, or a recessed plinth. Leg height, width, inset, and an optional shelf between the legs are adjustable.
- Freestanding placement and dragging magnetically align the cabinet back flush with a nearby straight wall in Grid or Lines snapping mode. Dragging uses the editor's shared 3D/2D move resolver; moving away releases the snap. The vanity stays on the floor as a child of the level.
- Wall-mounted models have no legs or plinth. **Floor clearance** sets the bottom of the cabinet; **Top height from floor** sets its top. Open Console has suspended side supports and a shelf. Placement snaps the cabinet back to either face of a straight wall and centers it at the pointer's height. In 3D, drag it horizontally and vertically along the wall while preserving the grab offset and cabinet size. Plan-view dragging preserves its elevation. It can transfer onto another nearby wall and follows its parent wall when the wall moves or rotates. Resizing keeps the back flush and constrains width to the wall length. Press R to switch wall faces.
- An optional countertop with adjustable thickness, overhang, edge profile, and backsplash.

Every part starts white, with no preassigned finish or texture. Use the editor's paint tool to apply materials independently to the fronts, carcass, drawer interiors/shelves, countertop/backsplash, legs/base, and handles/fittings. Paint assignments use the same `slots` and shared scene-material references as kitchen cabinets. The inspector contains no finish, color, or material controls; changing a design preset preserves paint assignments.

The **Corner** starter is a floor-standing five-sided cabinet with two perpendicular rear panels, an angled front, a recessed plinth, one or two doors, interior shelves, and an optional countertop. Adjust its equal wall-return lengths, height, plinth height, fronts, handles, and shelves in the inspector. Click individual doors or press E to animate them. Placement and 3D/2D dragging snap into nearby right-angle corners formed by joined straight wall endpoints in Grid or Lines mode. It remains a child of the level and can be moved freely away from a corner.

Choose **Round**, **Oval**, or **Rounded rectangle** with **Countertop** selected under **Basins**, then press **Place basin**. Placement raycasts the visible scene geometry in 3D, placing the basin base at the nearest surface under the cursor. Any item can support placement, including vanities, shelves, imported models, and ground surfaces. With no mesh hit, placement falls back to the ground grid. Grid snapping applies to that fallback; mesh hits follow the exact cursor intersection. Plan view casts downward through the cursor position to find the visible surface. The preview and click share the same placement calculation. Hover ghosts stay in the level frame, including above a moved or rotated vanity. Only clicking creates a basin and converts its pose into the vanity frame; hovering does not change any child lists. Hidden objects, invisible picking proxies, overlays, and the placement preview are ignored. A basin placed on a vanity becomes its child and follows the vanity when it moves or rotates. It remains individually selectable. Moving the basin within the vanity top keeps it attached; dropping it outside the top detaches it to the level while preserving its position and orientation. Other surfaces keep the basin as a child of the level. Duplicating a vanity includes its attached basins. **Base height from floor** remains available for manual adjustment.

The basin inspector controls width or round diameter, depth, bowl height, wall thickness, base taper, drain diameter, and an optional drain cover. The procedural shell has a hollow interior, a rounded rim, a sloped bowl floor, and a through drain opening. Bowl and drain cover start white and can be painted independently. Design changes preserve paint assignments. Overflow openings, plumbing, and countertop cutouts are deferred.

All basin shapes have UVs for tiled materials using the editor's convention of one UV unit per metre. The exterior/rim, inner wall, and drain bore use separate curved strips measured along the surface, with duplicated wrap vertices at the rear seam. The bowl floor, underside, and drain cover caps use planar mapping. UV island boundaries preserve the shell's smooth normals, and resizing regenerates the mapping with the geometry.

Select a basin to show its scene arrows. Drag them to change width or round diameter, depth for oval/rectangular bowls, and bowl height; the curved arrow rotates the basin. Width and depth resize around the center, while height keeps the base fixed on its support. Size arrows use millimetre precision within the inspector's dimension limits. The arrows follow the basin's local frame, including when it belongs to a rotated vanity. Detailed bowl and drain settings remain in the inspector.

Each basin has an empty `basin-tap-target` group behind the bowl, 55 mm beyond its rear edge at the supporting surface elevation. Undermount targets sit at the countertop top. The target belongs to the basin and follows its full vanity hierarchy. A saved `tapTarget` local pose overrides this default. Basins support children and duplicate their subtree, ready for the tap models being added next.

Tap tools should use the exported `basinTapSnap` for both placement preview and creation: hovering basin geometry returns its target pose and basin parent ID; Alt bypasses the snap. Existing tap movement should call `basinTapMoveChanges` with the desired level-local pose and pointer's `altKey`, then commit the returned changes with `applyNodeChanges`. An attached tap stays a basin child even when dragged outside the bowl, and its move updates the saved target in the same transaction. Alt detaches to the containing level, preserving the tap's level pose and leaving the target at its last saved location. A free tap can snap back onto a basin when hovered again. These APIs are prepared and tested; the tap catalog, models, and pointer tool will consume them in the next step.

Choose **Undermount** under **Basins**, then choose Round, Oval, or Rounded rectangle and press **Place basin**. Click a freestanding, wall-mounted, or corner vanity countertop. Placement requires support around the full mounting flange and places the bowl beneath the countertop, attached as a vanity child. Bowl dimensions, mounting flange width, and drain cover are adjustable; the scene arrows change width/diameter, depth, bowl depth, and rotation. The flange and bowl share the white bowl paint slot and metre-based UVs.

A derived CSG opening cuts through the countertop to match the bowl's inner mouth. It updates from committed and live basin poses and sizes, including rotation and multiple basins. Leaving the supported top detaches the basin and closes its hole. Removing the basin restores the countertop; undoing removal derives the opening again. Cuts preserve the countertop's material and leave the cabinet geometry intact. Cutters are transient geometry, with no extra saved scene nodes. Undermount placement currently supports Bath Space vanity countertops; imported models and other countertop kinds need their own host integration.

Undermount bowls also reserve 10 mm of drawer clearance around their exterior. Drawer bottoms, sides, backs, inner fronts, and slides are trimmed against the bowl's full relative sweep over the opening travel, with interior walls lining the resulting U-shaped recess. Multiple bowls share the union of their recesses. Drawer fronts and handles remain intact. If the front would collide or less than 20% of the box footprint remains usable, the drawer is held closed and the vanity inspector explains the obstruction. Basin movement, resizing, detachment, visibility changes, and removal rebuild the recesses from the original boxes. Cabinet plumbing and pipe clearances are not yet modelled.

Choose **Drop-in** under **Basins** for an inset basin. Round, Oval, and Rounded rectangle bowls are available. Click a Bath Space vanity countertop to place: the bowl hangs below its top, and a raised rim rests above the counter. Rim overhang and rim height are adjustable in the inspector. The automatic opening clears the bowl's outer edge by 2 mm on each side, with the wider rim covering the clearance. Placement aligns with the vanity, shows a tinted preview through the uncut countertop, and attaches only on click. Drop-in basins use the shared metre-scale UVs, paint slots, dimension arrows, tap target, cutout restoration, and drawer clearance logic. Other countertop kinds and imported hosts are not yet supported for inset cuts.

Basins remain separate objects. Undermount and drop-in basins cut the countertop; cabinet plumbing cutouts are still deferred; drawer boxes reserve bowl clearance. Existing dimension-only vanity nodes acquire defaults for the added parameters.

Reference designs: [Kohler freestanding cabinetry](https://www.kohler.com/en/products/vanities/shop-freestanding-vanities), [Winnow tapered legs and optional shelf](https://la.kohler.com/en/product-detail/33581-ASB?skuid=K-33581-ASB-0), [Bayliss fluted fronts and mixed storage](https://www.signaturehardware.com/72-in-bayliss-vanity---stoneware-white---vanity-cabinet-only/497807.html), and [Kohler's tailored vanity brochure with split top drawers](https://resources.kohler.com/webassets/kpna/brochures/TailoredVanites_US_FINAL.pdf).

Run `bun run check-types` and `bun run test` from this package to check the plugin and its geometry.

Choose **Semi-recessed** under **Basins** for a bowl that projects beyond the vanity front. Placement aligns the bowl with the front edge while checking its rear support. Front projection (40–180 mm) and recess depth (30–100 mm) are adjustable in the inspector. The countertop gets an open front notch; obstructing cabinet fronts and drawer parts get clearance cuts. Affected hinged doors stay closed. Removing the basin restores the source geometry. The bowl retains shared UVs, paint, dimension arrows, tap targets, and click-only parent attachment.

Choose **Wall hung** under **Basins** for four designs based on the supplied references: Sculpted shroud, Slim rectangle, Classic curved, and Deep box. They have distinct ceramic aprons, an integrated rear tap deck, and a hollow bowl below the mounting plane. Sculpted shroud hides a bottle trap inside a wall-flush ceramic cover. Slim rectangle has an exposed bottle trap, Classic curved has a curved waste trap and raised rear lip, and Deep box has a ribbed flexible waste pipe. Every assembly connects the drain to a wall flange at the rear mounting plane. Click a straight wall to place in 3D or plan view; keep clicking to add separate basins. Esc ends placement. The basin becomes a wall child and follows wall movement and rotation. Move it along the wall or onto another wall; 3D dragging adjusts its height, while plan dragging preserves it. Hidden, curved, and undersized walls reject new placement.

The inspector controls width, projection from the wall, bowl depth, wall thickness, taper, drain, and top height from the floor. The default top height is 850 mm. Size arrows keep the rear edge flush with the wall. Press R or use **Switch wall face** to move a selected basin to the other face. Bowl, deck, and shroud share a white ceramic paint slot. Drain covers and waste fittings start in chrome and have separate paint slots; the overflow recess also has its own slot. The tap target sits on the rear deck and follows the wall hierarchy. Basins support tap children and subtree duplication. The Waste pipe inspector can hide the assembly, switch between concealed, bottle, curved, and flexible waste, and adjust the outlet drop below the bowl. Geometry and floor clearance update with the basin size and mounting height. An optional overflow recess and trim provide a visual detail; they do not bore a plumbing channel through the shell. The tap target sits 60 mm from the wall on the rear deck. Tap holes and mounting brackets are not modelled.

Semi-recessed basins use their own ceramic shell rather than the vessel geometry: a tall projecting apron, round-front/soft oval/rectangular designs, and an integrated rear tap deck with a 35 mm bore. The deck and bowl are one closed mesh, with no extra plane or separate flange. The default tap target is on the deck; the drain follows the forward-offset bowl. Geometry, plan outline, counter cut and storage clearance use the same semi-recessed footprint. Design references: Roca The Gap A3270YG000 (550 × 420 × 160 mm) and Hindware Enigma (520 × 460 × 160 mm).

The Taps catalog has 15 countertop and 2 wall-mounted reference-inspired designs built entirely from new Three.js geometry. No reference GLBs or thumbnails are included. Arc, lever, square, and traditional taps share parametric construction; the spring pullout, sculpted tap, plate spout, and wall mixer have distinct assemblies. Omitted dimensions use the chosen preset; switching designs in the inspector restores that design's defaults. Countertop origins sit on the mounting surface, and wall fixtures project toward negative Z from the local wall plane.

Every tap has a cartridge housing and a pivoting handle. Choose lever, pin, cross or wheel handles and adjust their length, thickness and opening angle. The inspector also controls spout thickness, outlet drop, base shape and dimensions, aerator grilles, decorative rings and temperature markers. Pullout taps expose spring turns, spring radius, spray-head length and a spray button; wall mixers expose fitting spacing. Taps start white and expose separate body, trim and handle slots to the editor paint tool. Selected taps show height, reach and body-thickness arrows plus the existing move control. Resizing preserves the mounting origin and attachment.

Catalog thumbnails are rendered directly from `buildTapGeometry`. Run `bun run thumbnails` in this package and open the printed local URL to regenerate them and inspect the model gallery. Choose a tap card to start placement. Hover any basin to snap the preview to its tap target, then click to create a tap child. Each basin has one tap slot. Clicking an occupied basin atomically replaces its previous tap, with undo restoring it. Keep clicking to fill other basins; Escape cancels. Empty space and unrelated surfaces do not create taps. Attached taps follow their basin and its updated target, including dimension edits. The same snapping works in plan view.

Moving an existing countertop, undermount, drop-in or semi-recessed basin uses the same surface resolver as creation in both 3D and plan view. Moves can transfer between vanities and detach to ground, preserving the basin ID and its children. Semi-recessed bowls snap back to the vanity front; inset bowls retain their mounting elevation over existing cutouts. The drag excludes the moved subtree from raycasting, leaves scene nodes unchanged until commit, and Esc restores the original item. A committed move updates parent child lists and derived counter/storage cuts; undo restores the prior attachment.

Every basin geometry contains one empty `basin-tap-target` group, tagged with `attachmentTarget: 'tap'` and the basin ID. Vessel, drop-in, and undermount targets are centered 55 mm behind the bowl; undermount targets sit at the supporting countertop top. Semi-recessed targets coincide with the rear deck tap bore and follow bowl height and recess depth. Wall-hung targets sit halfway across the usable rear deck, accounting for bowl shape and the classic design's raised rear lip. Targets recalculate from basin dimensions when geometry rebuilds; an undermount system also synchronizes parent-dependent elevation during live vanity edits. Explicitly saved custom target poses remain fixed in the basin's local frame.

Choose **Basin → Full pedestal** for Classic full pedestal, Square full pedestal or Tapered monobloc. These attach directly to a straight wall and touch the current level floor; no vanity is required. New placement and moving reuse the wall placement tool. The basin rim follows total height, and changing height rebuilds the pedestal to keep its foot on the floor. Width, depth and total-height arrows are available, with separate basin/pedestal paint slots and metre-scale UVs. Each model includes an empty `basin-tap-target` on its rear tap deck and supports the existing tap attachment APIs. The hollow pedestal has rear plumbing access.

Tap slots use `slotId: "tap"` and `capacity: 1` on the target group. Placement and attachment helpers replace occupants across all basin types in both 3D and plan view, preserving other basin children. Hover previews temporarily hide the old tap and restore it on cancellation. Taps cannot be duplicated independently into an occupied slot; duplicating a basin still carries its tap subtree.

Choose **Basin → Half pedestal** for Classic half pedestal, Square half pedestal and Integrated tapered shroud. The basin attaches directly to a straight wall, with no vanity dependency. Its short hollow shroud conceals the waste area and follows the chosen mounting height. Mounting height and bowl/shroud dimensions are adjustable; arrows edit width, depth and shroud height. New placement and moving use the same wall resolver in 3D and plan view. Models have metre-scale UVs, separate bowl/shroud paint slots, and a shared empty `basin-tap-target` on the rear tap deck.


Basins expose **Tap mounting → Single-hole / Three-hole set** and bounded hot-to-cold spacing. The three-hole mixer is one tap item, with one centre spout and separate hot/cold controls. Its three empty physical mounting points share one logical replacement slot. Selecting, moving, deleting or replacing the tap affects the complete assembly. Placement switches the basin layout in the same scene transaction; manual layout switching chooses a compatible default tap while preserving its ID and paint assignments. Undo restores both the layout and occupants. Legacy independent tap slots are collapsed when a complete set is installed or the mounting layout is changed.

Integrated semi-recessed, wall-hung and pedestal decks have real bores matching the selected layout. Vessel, drop-in and undermount mounting holes are derived cuts in a supporting vanity countertop. Moving or deleting the basin restores the counter; resizing keeps the separate handles and mounting points within the usable width. Arbitrary imported supporting surfaces are not cut automatically.


Selecting a bath fixture opens a **Section A-A** card at the top of its inspector. The card shows a plan with the centre cut and a dimensioned schematic section. All seven basin types, freestanding/wall-mounted/corner vanities, taps, toilets, shower arms, bathtubs, and flush controls use this card. **Section details** exposes component dimensions such as shell thickness, drains, pedestals, countertop construction, tap assemblies, cisterns, shower bends, and bath rims when those parts are enabled. Basin and shower drawings default to fitting the fixture with a marked floor break; **Floor context** shows the full elevation at a smaller scale. Drag the dots to preview dimensions in the drawing and 3D view; releasing saves one edit through the fixture’s existing placement and attachment logic. Escape, pointer cancellation, losing focus, or changing selection discards the drag preview. Metre inputs preview each valid typed value in the drawing and mesh, then save once on Enter or blur. Escape discards the numeric preview. Both input and pointer previews rebuild affected geometry without changing saved scene nodes. Drawing scales stay fixed across dimension edits so size changes remain visible. Metre inputs accept precise values within the existing bounds; arrow keys adjust focused handles by one step, Shift uses ten steps, and Home/End select the limits. Round vessel basins expose a diameter rather than an unused depth. Pedestal basins also expose total or shroud height. Click an item in the catalog’s **Placed** tab to select it. Hide collapses the card while keeping the other inspector controls available. These are simplified fixture sections, without host countertops, plumbing details, or fabrication tolerances.


Choose **Toilet → Wall hung toilets** for Rounded, D-shaped, Soft square, Compact or Elongated bowls. Click a straight wall in either 3D or plan view to place a toilet; use the Placed tab to select it. Every bowl supports a concealed cistern with a flush plate, an attached external cistern, a separate low-level external cistern, or a high-level external cistern with a pull chain. An attached tank adds its depth to the overall wall projection. Separate cisterns have a visible connecting flush pipe. Concealed cisterns are represented by their visible flush plate; the host wall is not cut or rebuilt.

The item inspector controls bowl width, projection, height, ceramic thickness, taper, rimless construction, seat thickness, seat visibility, lid visibility and opening, tank arrangement and dimensions, external tank elevation, flush pipe diameter, mounting height and wall face. Bowl edits preserve the chosen mounting height. Bowl, seat/lid, external tank and hardware have separate paint slots. Moving and changing projection keep the rear mounting plane attached to either wall face. Catalog search, favorites and Placed include toilets.

Toilets use the editable **Section A-A** card. Its plan places the transverse cut through the bowl centre, including the extra projection of an attached tank. The section shows the ceramic shell, seat opening, lid and actual floor clearance; tanks and external pipes behind the cut are schematic elevation details. Flush plates and cistern controls have their own section cards. Bowl width, projection, body height and rim height can be edited with section handles or metre inputs. These generic models and drawings are for layout planning, rather than manufacturer-specific plumbing or fabrication details.

Choose **Shower** for wall-mounted shower arms. Round arms have straight, angled, elbow, curved and gooseneck shapes; square arms have straight, angled and sharp elbow shapes. Click a straight wall to place, or drag an existing arm along a wall in 3D or plan view. Item settings control projection, tube diameter or square width, outlet drop, arch rise, bend radius, outlet angle, connector length, mounting height, flange shape, size and thickness. Shape-specific controls appear only when relevant. Arm, flange and outlet connector have independent white paint slots.

Each arm includes one empty `showerhead_target_shower-head` group at the outlet, with `attachmentTarget: 'showerhead'`, `slotId: 'shower-head'`, host identity and capacity 1. The slot identity survives shape and dimension changes; its local position and rotation follow the outlet. A future shower head stores the arm as `parentId` and `shower-head` as `slotId`. The arm system resolves that child's local transform from the current slot without creating history entries. The head's inlet belongs at its origin, with spray along local -Y. Shower head catalog and placement are a subsequent component.


Flush controls are separate scene items for every toilet style. **Toilet → Wall flush plate** offers rectangular, rounded, square, round and oval plates. Place a plate on either wall face independently of a toilet. Select it to change its surround, round/rectangular/oval button shapes, single/dual/touchless operation, dimensions, mounting height and wall station. Plates retain their own wall position when a linked toilet moves or is deleted. Each plate has beveled edges, a recessed backing and fine button reveals. Edge radius and reveal width are editable. Plates have surround, button, sensor and backing/reveal paint slots and an editable Section A-A card.

Placing a toilet creates its matching control in the same undo step. External cisterns use a separately selectable **Cistern flush control** attached to the toilet, with top, side or pull-chain mounting, independent surround/button shapes, offset and chain-length settings. Controls follow their cistern dimensions; a tank control is hidden while its toilet uses a concealed tank. Use **Separate flush controls** in the toilet inspector to select, remove or add controls. Deleting a control leaves the toilet in place. Existing saved toilets are converted once, preserving old flush plate placement, hidden state, flush mode and hardware finishes; the conversion does not add an undo step.


Choose **Bath** for freestanding **Oval soaking bath**, **Rounded rectangle**, and **Slipper bath** presets. Click the floor in 3D or plan view to place, and press Esc to end placement. The hollow procedural shell has a rounded rim, sloping backrests, a through drain opening with an optional cover, and optional overflow trim. The slipper raises one end. Select the bath to edit length, width, rim height, bowl depth, and rim width, or use its size and rotation arrows. Its Section A-A card supports dimension previews. Shell, drain, and overflow have separate paint slots and metre-scale UVs.

Bath fittings default to **Wall mounted**. The empty `bath-wall-tap-target` behind the bowl is a service target. A nearby wall tap keeps its wall parent and can occupy the bath's single `tap` slot. Its along-wall and height offsets follow bath movement and resizing. Removing the bath preserves the wall tap at its last resolved position and clears its service link. **On the rim** reserves at least 90 mm of rear rim and exposes an empty `bath-tap-target`. Existing single-hole countertop taps can snap to that slot, replace its occupant atomically, and follow the bath hierarchy. Three-hole sets are not supported on the bath rim yet. **No target** disables new fitting attachment. Existing fitted taps are preserved when changing mounting mode.

These first baths use the existing tap catalog. Dedicated bath fillers, hand showers, tap-hole bores, overflow plumbing channels, water simulation, and built-in bath decks remain future work. See [bath research and implementation order](doc/bath-options-research.md).

### Floor standing toilets

Choose **Toilet → Floor standing toilets** for Back to wall, Compact back to wall, Square back to wall, Close coupled, One piece or Traditional pedestal. Place against either face of a straight wall in 2D or 3D. Each model has a floor-supported skirt or pedestal and an adjustable rim height. The one-piece design includes a ceramic bridge beneath the attached tank; the close-coupled design keeps the bowl and tank visually separate.

Settings cover bowl dimensions, foot width/depth, skirt or pedestal base, ceramic thickness, taper, rimless bowl, seat and lid, and concealed/attached/low-level/high-level cisterns. Flush controls remain separate selectable items. Section A-A includes the support down to the floor, with editable foot and cistern dimensions. Floor standing models participate in search, favorites, Placed, paint and undo. [Reference captures and sources](docs/references/floor-standing-toilets/README.md) document the visual starting point.

The floor-standing catalog also includes four **Two-piece close coupled** models (round front, comfort elongated, skirted and classic) and four additional **One-piece** models (compact, low profile, soft square and sculpted). Their tank proportions, base profiles and ceramic construction differ. The attached-cistern settings expose construction type, tank corner radius, taper, lid thickness and the two-piece tank joint gap. Section A-A exposes the corresponding dimensions and follows the body and cistern geometry; independent flush buttons follow lid-thickness edits. [Reference screenshots and eight-model showroom graph](docs/references/coupled-toilets/README.md) are included.

Wall placement uses the editor’s shared pointer tracker. The ghost follows wall hits in the wall parent coordinate frame; changing walls or faces, or leaving a wall, clears the previous grab offset. Wall-mounted items follow cursor height, with grid snapping when enabled. Floor-supported fixtures retain their floor anchoring.

Choose **Shower → Draw shower divider**. There is one divider tool; customize rows and columns after drawing. Click a start and endpoint to draw a framed glass partition, then keep clicking for connected segments when the wall continuation setting is Room. Single wall mode resets after each segment. Joining existing walls or dividers, or closing the chain, also resets the draft. **Enter** finishes the chain; **R** switches line/rectangle; **Esc** cancels the draft, then exits. Placement works in 2D, 3D and split view with wall-style snapping and an **Alt** bypass. Select a divider to edit length, height, rows, columns, frame profiles and glass thickness. Frame and glass can be painted separately. These are fixed partitions; doors and fabrication hardware are deferred. See [construction references and implementation notes](docs/shower-divider-research.md).

## Parametric shower items

The Shower catalog groups shape variants into component families. Place a Hand shower mount, then select it to choose a wall holder, supply outlet or slide rail, with round/square shape and optional supply, adjustment lever and shelf settings. Other shower shapes remain in their item settings panels. Every Shower component has a geometry-derived Section A-A drawing with editable dimensions. See [catalog grouping, settings and section behavior](docs/shower-parametric-catalog.md).
